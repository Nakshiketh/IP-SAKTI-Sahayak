# PART 5 — Banned, and what to do instead

## 5.1 Banned visual patterns

- Cream or parchment background near `#F4F1EA` with a serif display and a terracotta accent near
  `#D97757` — the strongest single tell
- Gradients of any kind, including gradient text and borders
- Glassmorphism and frosted panels
- Neon, glow, particles, floating orbs
- Identical rounded cards with the same `rgba(0,0,0,.1)` shadow for every content type
- One `border-radius` on everything
- A coloured rounded square with an icon above every card heading
- ALL-CAPS letter-spaced eyebrow labels
- Meta strings joined with middle dots as decoration
- `→` appended to link and button text
- One word in a headline coloured or italicised for emphasis
- Fade-and-slide-up on every section as you scroll
- Hover lift-and-shadow on every card
- Emoji as interface icons
- Symmetrical three-column grids for content that is not three parallel things
- Vector illustrations of people, robots, brains, circuit boards, or shields with checkmarks
- Stock photography of labs, herbs in bowls, or people pointing at screens
- Invented statistics, testimonials, logos or "trusted by" strips
- Dark mode built only because it looks technical, with contrast that fails in it

## 5.2 Banned copy

seamlessly · effortlessly · unlock · empower · revolutionise · transform · harness · leverage ·
cutting-edge · state-of-the-art · game-changing · next-generation · robust · powerful · intuitive ·
delve · navigate the complexities · in today's fast-paced world · exclamation marks in interface copy ·
headings that are rhetorical questions the page then answers · sentences describing the product's
importance rather than its behaviour · any claim the code cannot perform.

**The replacement test:** if a sentence would work verbatim on a website about a completely different
product, delete it and write one that would not.

## 5.3 What to do instead

- **Let content set the layout.** A statute comparison is a table. A decision flow is a numbered
  sequence. Reference material is dense, scannable and indexed. Professional legal and regulatory
  sites are text-forward; that density is the credibility signal.
- **Vary the rhythm.** If every section is heading → subheading → three cards, the page reads as
  generated even when each section is fine individually. Alternate: a full-width worked example, a
  dense comparison, a single quiet statement, a table.
- **Spend boldness once.** One memorable element — the manuscript-to-graph hero. Everything else
  quiet. Two competing focal points and both fail.
- **Colour carries meaning.** Indigo means sourced. Lac red means caution or declining to answer.
  Green is structure and primary action. Never decorative.
- **Numbers only for sequences.** The classification flow and the pipeline are sequences. Feature
  lists are not.
- **Motion answers actions.** Drawers, expansions, streaming, step advancement. Not scroll decoration.
- **Write like a practitioner.** "Whether you can patent it depends on what the drug regulator calls
  it" does more for credibility than any adjective.
- **Show the limits prominently.** Amateur products hide what they cannot do. The abstention states,
  the "not legal advice" pill, the "records are not authority" label and honest evaluation numbers are
  the strongest professionalism signals in the build.
